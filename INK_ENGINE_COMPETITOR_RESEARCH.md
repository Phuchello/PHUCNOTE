# PHUCNOTE handwriting competitor evidence ledger

Research date: 2026-10-11. Scope: public documentation and first-person reports; no competitor application was installed or benchmarked in this checkpoint. This ledger supports hypotheses for an independent engine, not a claim of equivalent handwriting quality. Concept A — Academic Atelier remains the future UI direction.

## How to interpret the evidence

**Documented** means a first-party source describes the behavior. It does not establish measured latency, correctness on every device, or implementation details. **Marketing** means an unquantified quality/performance claim. **Anecdotal** means a named user's account, with no controlled reproduction. **Unknown** means the reviewed sources do not establish it; it does not mean the feature is absent.

The six products' public material reviewed here does not disclose a reproducible ink engine implementation, pressure-normalization formula, stabilizer coefficients, tessellation algorithm, or measured Y700/AP501U pen-to-display latency. Concepts documents more brush semantics than the others, but those semantics are not source code. Goodnotes' public discussion of handwriting recognition concerns recognition rather than wet-ink rendering; it cannot identify its rendering algorithm. [Goodnotes engineering interview](https://www.goodnotes.com/blog/machine-learning-engineer-handwriting-recognition)

## What the sources cannot establish

| Application | Unknowns that screenshots and public feature descriptions cannot settle | Independent PHUCNOTE experiment suggested by the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Goodnotes | Android availability of every shared-guide slider; filtering coefficients; tip behavior on short accents; lift-off correction; Y700 latency. | Compare fixed-width and pressure-sensitive ink using identical input; separately score caps, short diacritics and wet/completed consistency. Keep stabilization configurable and measurable. |
| Notewise | Prediction versus filtering implementation; latency-level internals; calligraphy nib orientation; Y700 compatibility of low-latency paths. | Sweep smoothing settings while measuring endpoint lag and corner/diacritic loss, rather than choosing the smoothest static picture. Require a device-specific fallback for any low-latency experiment. |
| Samsung Notes | Pressure curve of each pen; proprietary prediction; S Pen behavior on Lenovo hardware; pen-to-display timing under density/thermal load. | Preserve rapid favorite switching and test pressure plateaus/ramp response independently of whether a pen looks attractive in a screenshot. A Y700 installation must be officially supported before it is a fair comparator. |
| Noteshelf 3 | Exact NS3 Android palm-rejection controls, pressure/tilt curves, live-to-final changes, actual zoom-box coordinate fidelity. | Test zoomed small marks, hand-rest input and coordinate preservation separately. A zoom box is a future usability idea, not part of this engine checkpoint. |
| Flexcil | Pressure mapping, stabilizer details, vector tessellation, whether gesture arbitration adds latency or drops points. | Keep explicit input modes and test finger navigation during stylus gestures. Include constant-pressure, dense annotations and highlighter self-overlap in the benchmark. |
| Concepts | Native low-latency implementation, complete dynamics equations, which custom brush features reach Android, transfer fidelity to another engine. | Separate real pressure from velocity-derived width, expose normalized response only in the isolated prototype, and verify raster/vector geometry before proposing native transfer. |

These experiments are independently derived requirements. No proprietary code, screenshots, brush textures or branded UI are copied. A control with the same name can represent a different algorithm in each product; slider percentages are not portable calibration values.

## Implication for this checkpoint

The defensible target is measurable pen behavior: reliable dots and accents, controllable width dynamics, low wet-ink processing cost, stable lift-off, robust finger/stylus arbitration and exact saved-stroke fidelity. Vendor claims justify investigating these qualities but cannot select Flutter, Jetpack Ink or a hybrid. The architecture decision must use PHUCNOTE's reproducible A/B evidence and retain **NOT EXECUTED** for physical Y700/AP501U and same-device competitor comparisons until those runs actually happen.
